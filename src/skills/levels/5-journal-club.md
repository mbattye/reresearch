# Journal club

## Who you're writing for

A graduate student or researcher in a neighbouring area. They know the standard tools and
vocabulary of the wider field and read papers for a living. They want to know whether this one
is worth their time: what is actually new, how it was done, and how far to trust it.

## Language

- Use standard field terminology without defining it. Define only terms specific to this subfield
  or invented by this paper.
- Name methods, codes, datasets and models as the paper does ("GENIE", "PREM density profile",
  "three-flavour treatment with matter effects").
- Compact and exact. A clause of context, not a sentence.

## Numbers and maths

- Full precision as reported, with uncertainties and confidence levels.
- Unicode maths, including the central relation if the result rests on it (e.g. how the shift
  scales with density contrast and zone width). No LaTeX.
- State the parameter choices that drive the result (baselines, energies, priors, cuts).

## Comparisons

None. Compare with prior work and with theory instead.

## Shape

Hook under about 340 characters. 6-10 posts after the hook. The approach and caveat sections get the most room.

## What to dwell on

- What is new relative to the closest prior work, named.
- Method detail: the approximations, the parameter space explored, what was held fixed.
- Systematics and statistics: what dominates, and whether the paper treats it adequately.
- Where the result would or would not generalise.

## Avoid

- Restating textbook background.
- Taking the authors' framing of significance on trust; say what the evidence supports.

## Calibration

**Headline:** Matter-effect sensitivity to a 3× density fault zone at DUNE is 0.55 pp in
P(νμ→νe), far below what a precursor search needs

**Hook:** A three-flavour calculation with a 100 km, 3× density anomaly at mid-baseline shifts
P(νμ→νe) at 2.5 GeV by 0.55 pp (6.8% relative), nearly independent of δCP. A 3σ distinction
needs ~2×10³ appearance events per time bin against DUNE's 10³–10⁴ total over several years:
a feasibility no-go for week-scale seismic precursors.
