# Pub quiz

## Who you're writing for

An interested adult who left science at school. They read the news, like a good fact to repeat,
and will follow an explanation if it is clear. They do not know the field's vocabulary and do not
want a lecture.

## Language

- Plain English first. Introduce at most three technical terms per thread, each with a short
  plain gloss the first time ("neutrino oscillation, where a neutrino changes type in flight").
- Name the experiment or instrument and say in a phrase what it is ("DUNE, a neutrino experiment
  firing a beam 1,300 km from Illinois to South Dakota").
- Conversational but not chatty. Dry humour is fine when the material earns it.

## Numbers and maths

- Up to four numbers, rounded sensibly, with everyday units. Check any conversion or "times
  bigger" comparison against the paper's own values; drop it if unsure. Percentages and "times bigger" are
  better than scientific notation.
- No equations, scientific notation or compound units (no "10⁻⁹", no "GeV cm⁻² s⁻¹"), and no
  symbols beyond %, × and °C. When the headline result is a quantity like that, say what it
  means in words ("a ceiling on how many could be arriving") instead of quoting it.
- Say what the number means ("a shift of about half a percentage point, small enough that you
  need thousands of measurements to be sure it is real").

## Comparisons

Allowed sparingly: one at most, and only where it explains something a plain description cannot.

## Shape

Hook under about 260 characters. 4-6 posts after the hook: one or two for context, one for the method, one or two for results,
one caveat, one takeaway.

## What to dwell on

The question, the clever part of the method in one sentence, the main result with its single
most telling number, and the honest "so what".

## Avoid

- Jargon creeping back in through proper nouns. Explain what something is the first time.
- False certainty, and false drama. A careful "no" is a perfectly good story.

## Calibration

**Headline:** Neutrino beams could feel the rock change near a fault, but not fast enough to
forecast earthquakes

**Hook:** A dense, stressed fault zone in the path of the DUNE neutrino beam would change how
often neutrinos switch type by about half a percentage point. Spotting that takes thousands of
detections, far more than DUNE collects in the days before a quake.
