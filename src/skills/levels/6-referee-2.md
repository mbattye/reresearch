# Referee #2

## Who you're writing for

A specialist in this exact subfield, reading the way a careful referee would. They know the
literature, the standard methods and their failure modes. They want the claim, the evidence, and
an honest assessment of the gap between them, fast.

## Language

- Terse and technical. Field shorthand is fine. Define nothing that a specialist would know.
- Critical but fair: assess the work, not the authors. Credit what is done well, plainly.
- Separate clearly what the paper shows, what it assumes, and what it asserts.

## Numbers and maths

- Exact values, uncertainties, confidence levels and the parameter choices behind them.
- Unicode maths freely, including the key expression or scaling where it matters. No LaTeX.
- Flag numbers that depend on a single benchmark point or an optimistic assumption.

## Comparisons

None. Compare only with the literature and with competing methods.

## Shape

Hook under about 340 characters. 4-7 dense posts after the hook. Map the sections to a referee's reading:
- **context**: the claim and its novelty against the closest prior work;
- **approach**: method soundness and key approximations;
- **result**: what the numbers establish, at what significance;
- **caveat**: unexamined assumptions, missing checks, what a referee would ask for;
- **takeaway**: how strong the evidence is overall and what would change your mind.

## What to dwell on

Novelty, assumptions, robustness, missing systematics or cross-checks, and whether the
conclusion follows from the evidence.

## Avoid

- Criticisms you have not checked against the text. Before saying a paper lacks a scan, a
  systematic or a cross-check, make sure it is not there; missing-from-the-abstract is not
  missing-from-the-paper.
- Background a specialist already has.
- Snark, or criticism the paper does not warrant. A sound incremental result should be called
  sound and incremental.

## Calibration

**Headline:** A clean no-go: DUNE matter effects cannot resolve week-scale fault-zone density
changes

**Hook:** The 0.55 pp shift in P(νμ→νe) at 2.5 GeV is a best case (central 100 km zone, 3×
density) and is degenerate in width, density and position, so one baseline cannot locate the
anomaly. The event rate is a placeholder pending full DUNE simulation, but a week of data falls
over an order of magnitude short regardless.
