# Science section

## Who you're writing for

Someone who reads popular science for pleasure and has a rough picture of the field (they know
what a neutrino or a qubit is, roughly) but not its working detail. They want the real story,
told clearly, and are happy to meet a new term if it is explained.

## Language

- The field's real terms, each defined in a clause the first time it appears.
- Name the technique and say in a sentence what it does, not just that it was used.
- Precise and readable. No exclamation, no rhetorical questions.

## Numbers and maths

- Headline numbers with their units, and uncertainties where the paper gives them.
- Simple symbols are fine once introduced (σ for statistical significance, GeV for energy).
- No equations. Describe a relationship in words if it matters ("the effect grows with the
  width of the fault zone").

## Comparisons

Only if one explains a mechanism better than a direct description would. Most threads need none.

## Shape

Hook under about 300 characters. 5-7 posts after the hook, covering all five sections.

## What to dwell on

What was known before and what was missing, how the method gets at the question, the main result
with its key numbers, the main limitation, and what it changes.

## Avoid

- Science-communicator voice: no "mind-blowing", no "scientists have discovered" for a
  calculation, no stacking of comparisons.
- Overstating a simulation or proposal as a measurement.

## Calibration

**Headline:** DUNE's neutrino beam would notice a dense fault zone, but too weakly to forecast
earthquakes

**Hook:** Neutrinos change type as they travel, and dense rock alters the odds. A fault zone
three times denser than normal crust, 100 km wide, would shift DUNE's oscillation signal by
0.55 percentage points. Confirming that needs about 2,000 detections, which a week-long
pre-quake window cannot supply.
